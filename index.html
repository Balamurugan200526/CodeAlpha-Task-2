01 — Introduction

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a type of cyberattack where criminals disguise themselves as trusted entities to trick you into revealing sensitive information — like passwords, credit card numbers, or personal data.

🎣

The "Hook"

Attackers craft convincing fake messages that appear to come from banks, companies, or people you trust — to lure you into taking action.

🎭

Disguise & Deception

They copy logos, email styles, and website designs of real companies to make their fake messages look completely legitimate.

⚡

Urgency Tactics

Phrases like "Your account will be suspended!" or "Act now!" are designed to panic you into clicking without thinking carefully.

💰

The Goal

Steal passwords, credit card numbers, personal data, or install malware on your device — all through one click or one form submission.

02 — Types of Attacks

Common Phishing Types

📧

Email Phishing

Mass emails sent to thousands of people pretending to be banks, services like Netflix or Amazon, or government agencies. The most common type.

🎯

Spear Phishing

Targeted attack on a specific person using personal information gathered from LinkedIn, social media, or data breaches to appear more convincing.

📱

Smishing (SMS)

Fake text messages claiming to be from your bank, courier services, or government — containing malicious links to steal your data.

📞

Vishing (Voice)

Phone calls from "bank employees" or "tech support" asking for OTPs, passwords, or remote access to your computer. Very convincing.

👑

Whaling

Highly targeted phishing aimed at executives and CEOs — often impersonating board members or legal departments to authorize large wire transfers.

🌐

Clone Phishing

Attackers copy a real email you previously received, replace the link with a malicious one, and resend it — making it nearly identical to the original.

03 — Red Flags

How to Recognize a Phishing Email

Here is a real-world example of a phishing email with red flags highlighted:

From:support@paypa1-security.com ⚠ FAKE DOMAIN
To:you@gmail.com
Subject:URGENT: Your account has been suspended! ⚠ URGENCY

Dear Valued Customer, ⚠ NO NAME


We have detected suspicious activity on your PayPal account. Your account has been temporarily limited.


You must verify your information within 24 hours or your account will be permanently suspended. ⚠ FAKE DEADLINE


Click here to verify: http://paypa1-verify-login.xyz/secure ⚠ FAKE LINK


Regards,
PayPal Security Team

Spotted the red flags? Here's what to look for in any suspicious email:

  • Fake sender domain — "paypa1-security.com" instead of "paypal.com". Look closely at every character.
  • Generic greeting — "Dear Valued Customer" instead of your actual name. Real companies use your name.
  • Urgency and threats — "24 hours or your account will be suspended!" is designed to panic you.
  • Suspicious links — Hover over any link before clicking. The real URL shown at the bottom of your browser reveals the truth.
  • Grammar and spelling errors — Legitimate companies proofread their emails. Errors are a major red flag.
  • Unexpected attachments — Never open .exe, .zip, or .docm files from unknown senders.
  • Requests for sensitive info — Real banks and services NEVER ask for passwords or OTPs via email.
04 — Social Engineering

Tactics Attackers Use

Phishing is powered by psychology, not just technology. Here are the manipulation tactics attackers rely on:

😱

Fear

"Your account has been hacked. Act immediately to secure it."

⏰

Urgency

"You have 2 hours to verify or lose your account forever."

🏆

Greed

"Congratulations! You've won a ₹50,000 prize. Claim now."

🤝

Trust

Impersonating your boss, bank, or a government agency you trust.

🙏

Authority

"This is the IT Department. We need your login to fix a critical issue."

❤️

Sympathy

"I'm stranded abroad and need you to send money urgently."

05 — Best Practices

How to Stay Safe

01

Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Even if attackers steal your password, 2FA stops them from accessing your account.

02

Verify the sender's email address

Check every character of the sender's domain. "amaz0n.com" is not Amazon.

03

Hover before you click

Hover over any link to see the real URL in the status bar before clicking it.

04

Never share OTPs or passwords

No real bank, company, or IT team will ever ask for your OTP or password.

05

Check for HTTPS on websites

Before entering any information, ensure the site uses HTTPS and the domain is correct.

06

Keep software updated

Updates patch security vulnerabilities that phishing malware exploits.

07

Use a password manager

Password managers auto-fill only on the real website — they won't fill on fakes.

08

Report suspicious emails

Mark phishing emails as spam and report them to your IT team or email provider.
